Printable parts

Lens ringsDrag to turn, scroll or pinch to zoom.

  1. Ring A (bottom, gray): 36 mm across with a 25 mm opening, 1.6 mm thick, with 4 pins that stick up.
  2. Film (black): lay a piece of thin black polyethylene (4 mil sheeting or a heavy trash bag) over ring A and press it down onto the pins so they poke through.
  3. Ring B (top, gold): press it straight down. Its outer skirt slides over ring A and the barb snaps under ring A's bottom edge for good. The pins go into a groove on its underside, so it fits at any angle.
  4. Trim the extra film around the outside with scissors.

Print both flat on a normal printer (0.4 mm nozzle, 0.2 mm layers), PETG or PLA, no supports: ring A pins up, ring B upside down (flat top on the bed). Sizes are a first try; if the snap is too tight or loose, tell me and I'll change the gap.

What's in it

  1. Mount (purple): a wide plate screwed up under the table for good with 9 screws (4 down each side, 1 in the middle). It's the box's lid, with a C-channel down each side: the box is open on top, and a lip along each of its top edges slides into the C's like a drawer until it hits the stop at the back. The wood screws sit countersunk and flush inside the C's (4 a side, 1 in the middle), with holes in the bottom of each C so a screwdriver reaches straight in. One M3 screw through the side of the right C locks the box into a nut in the lip, so taking the box in and out never touches the table screws.
  2. One long box, front to back: the Apple plug housing at the front, the strip in the middle, then a foil partition, then the Wi-Fi board and heat sensor at the back. It's wide enough to get a hand in and pull a plug, with room on both sides so the power cord and the USB-C cable bend in a wide curve instead of getting smashed.
  3. Thin floor: no separate tray. The strip sits right on the floor. It slides into a cradle: vented walls rising from the floor on both sides, braced by L-shaped gussets, with thin strips across the top that hold it down. No ceiling and no screws, so the box is shorter. The cube sits on a hollow, ribbed cradle with a foam pad.
  4. Back plate: slides down into slots right against the back of the strip and holds it in place. Open notches in its bottom edge slide down over the power cord and the USB-C cable.
  5. Thermal wall (foil plate): a second slide-in plate half an inch behind the back plate, foil facing the strip. Its cord and USB-C notches are offset from the back plate's, so the cables zigzag and the strip's heat has no straight path to the sensor.
  6. Front housing: a little wider than the Apple 20 W cube, with finger room inside on both sides so you can reach in and pluck the cube out. The cube plugs into the strip's end outlet, its USB-C port facing the player.
  7. Front bezel slides up into a track on the front of the box and screws in at its two top corners into square M3 nuts. A wide opening lets any USB-C cable plug in. Foam pads inside it and on the cradle squeeze the cube so it fits tight.
  8. SUPERDANNY flat-plug strip (white, like the real one) in the middle, its end outlet facing front: 2 outlets on its face (with the green and red safety lights), facing down (the front one is open through the bottom so you can use it), 1 on each end, and the USB ports on one side, about mid-length and toward the table side: A1, A2, C1, C2 from the outlet end. The two keyhole slots are on the table side. It's mounted outlets-down, like under a desk. Its flat cord comes out of the dead centre of the long side opposite the USB ports, runs back and down to the floor, through the back plate's notch, then S-curves across the half-inch gap to the USB side, out through the thermal wall's notch next to the USB-C cable, and back along that wall, past a zip-tie slot, out the rear to the table's power strip.
  9. Sensor pod: the bottom plate behind the thermal wall, with the lens, the heat sensor and the Wi-Fi board on it as one piece. Four screws go up into two struts (one right behind the thermal wall, one at the back). Unscrew it, drop it out, unplug the USB-C, and work on it on the bench.
  10. XIAO ESP32C3 Wi-Fi board (blue) on the pod, powered from the strip's USB port by a short right-angle cable.
  11. GY-906 BCC heat sensor (black tube, narrow view) on two posts on the pod, angled forward, looking down through a funnel and the lens toward the player's lap. If it can't see enough, we drop it lower.
  12. Lens (round IR window in the pod): two rings snap together with black polyethylene film between them; pins on one ring poke through the film into the other. Set back so it clears the thermal wall.
  13. Open vent slots along both sides. (The NFC sign-in sticker goes on the tabletop, not the module.)
